# Philosophy of the project

## History

We've been working on this concept since 2012, testing and changing prototypes and teams, and now — after many tests, mock-ups, and 2 beta versions — we have a better design and understanding of what Helpbuttons should be.

During the 2021 COVID crisis, the team could make some tests and improved the first prototype. They also realized the urgent need for better social cooperation tools. If we want communities to be resilient and prepared, we need to cultivate connections and social integration every day, not just in crisis moments.

Collaborative apps require a large amount of technical support and design effort that can't be sustained by a single-purpose project. We see that many useful collaborative apps fail because they serve just one commitment or one small community — the work required makes them unsustainable. Building an app to help elders in your town is useful, but it probably won't get enough economic and professional support to survive long-term. The same problem is being solved in parallel by many others — for food sharing, transport sharing, house sharing, skill exchange, and countless other purposes. Why not unify the effort?

We think it's possible. We analyzed the common structure of collaborative apps and designed a base skeleton with the core fields they all share, plus a modular layer that can be adapted to any purpose. We need people to help refine these designs, sustain community support, and develop modules that extend the base for more use cases.

---

## Where do we want to go?

Helpbuttons aims to become a tool for coordinating social cooperation, especially in communities where individuals may not know each other well. Neighborhoods, towns, valleys, regions, associations, and more could use Helpbuttons to collaborate in sharing food, playing sports, mapping resources, selling objects, requesting special assistance, and more.

The ideal state of Helpbuttons is **a network of networks** — instances that federate to share users and content between them, facilitating cooperation and mutual support across communities without centralized control.

## Core values

### Digital autonomy

Every Helpbuttons instance is self-hosted and self-governed. Communities have full control over their data, their moderation policies, their look and feel, and their rules. There is no central platform extracting data, no algorithmic feed optimized for engagement, and no corporate entity deciding what content is visible.

Digital autonomy means: **your community, your server, your rules**.

This also means communities can leave, fork, or run their own version of the software at any time. The code is open, the data is portable, and no one holds a lock.

### Cooperation over competition

The collaborative economy has largely been captured by platforms that extract value from communities while providing the coordination infrastructure. Helpbuttons is built on the opposite premise: the infrastructure itself should belong to the community.

We believe that connecting people around shared needs — not extracting profit from their interactions — is both possible and necessary. The technical architecture reflects this: buttons, networks, and users are designed to flow between communities, not to be siloed for retention.

### Inclusion and accessibility

A tool for social cooperation is only useful if it reaches the people who most need cooperation. We aim for Helpbuttons to be usable by people who are not technically sophisticated, to be translatable into any language, and to be deployable on modest infrastructure.

Specific design choices that reflect this:
- Moderation can be distributed across multiple admins, not locked to a single person
- Invitations allow private networks to grow through trusted social networks
- Location can be hidden to protect privacy while still enabling geo-based discovery
- The platform ships with support for multiple languages out of the box

### Federation

Federation is both a technical feature and a philosophical commitment. We do not want to create another silo. A Helpbuttons instance should be able to share users and content with other instances, letting communities that are geographically or socially adjacent cooperate without having to merge into a single platform.

The model is inspired by the fediverse: independent communities that can interoperate, with each community retaining full sovereignty over its own data and governance.

### Sustainability through openness

Proprietary platforms can be shut down, acquired, or pivoted. Open-source projects with broad contributor communities are more resilient. By making Helpbuttons open, we invite developers, translators, community organizers, and designers from around the world to improve and maintain it together.

The project's long-term sustainability depends on the community of contributors — voluntary programmers, translators, designers, and community organizers who believe in open tools for social cooperation.

## The problem we are solving

Platforms like Airbnb, BlaBlaCar, and Wallapop coordinate cooperation effectively, but:

- They own all the data
- They optimize for profit, not for community wellbeing
- They can be shut down or changed unilaterally
- They cannot be adapted to specific community needs
- Small communities cannot afford to build their own version

Helpbuttons solves this by providing the shared infrastructure — the hard parts of building a geo-based collaboration platform — as a commons. Any community can take it, adapt it, and run it for their own purposes.
